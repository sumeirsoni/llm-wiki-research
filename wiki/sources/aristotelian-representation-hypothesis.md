# Revisiting the Platonic Representation Hypothesis: An Aristotelian View

## Summary

Gröger, Wen, and Brbić show that raw representational similarity scores are systematically inflated by model **width** (embedding dimension relative to sample size) and **depth** (max-over-layer search). They introduce a metric-agnostic permutation null-calibration framework that yields principled zero points and valid p-values, then revisit the Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH). After calibration, global spectral convergence across vision and language largely disappears, while local neighborhood overlap (mKNN, cycle-kNN, CKNNA) retains scaling-dependent cross-modal alignment. They propose the **Aristotelian Representation Hypothesis**: models converge on shared local neighborhood relationships ("who is near whom"), not global geometry or exact local distances.

## Key Contributions

- **Width confounder**: spectral and geometric metrics (CKA, RV, Procrustes) have O(d/n) null baselines under independence; wider models appear more aligned even with no true signal.
- **Depth confounder**: max-over-layer aggregation inflates scores as O(σ√log M); deeper models appear more aligned because more layer pairs are searched.
- **Null-calibration framework**: permutation-based scalar and aggregation-aware calibration works for any bounded similarity metric with finite-sample Type-I control.
- **PRH re-evaluation**: reproduces Huh et al. (2024) on 204 vision–language pairs (WIT, n=1024) and extends to video–language (VideoMAE + PVD).
- **Aristotelian Representation Hypothesis (ARH)**: calibrated evidence supports local topological convergence, not global spectral convergence.
- **Locality characterization**: mKNN alignment persists across k; CKA-RBF with small bandwidth shows no calibrated alignment, separating neighborhood identity from metric distances.

## Methodology

The paper formalizes representational similarity s(X, Y) across spectral (CKA linear/RBF, CCA/SVCCA/PWCCA, RV), neighborhood (mKNN, cycle-kNN, CKNNA), and geometric (RSA, Procrustes) families. Under H₀ (independence), null scores are generated by permuting sample correspondences in Y.

**Scalar calibration** computes a critical threshold τ_α from K=200 permutations and rescales: s_cal = max((s_obs − τ_α)/(s_max − τ_α), 0).

**Aggregation-aware calibration** applies the same procedure to the aggregate statistic T(S) (e.g., max over all layer pairs), generalizing maxT resampling to arbitrary metrics.

Experiments follow Huh et al. (2024): BLOOM/OpenLLaMA/LLaMA vs ImageNet-21K/MAE/DINOv2/CLIP families, max-over-layer reporting, Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction. Synthetic controls vary n, d, noise distribution, and injected low-rank signal.

## Key Results

- Under H₀, uncalibrated CKA/RV/Procrustes drift with d/n; calibrated scores collapse to zero across Gaussian, Student-t, and Laplace noise.
- Type-I error stays at or below α=0.05; detection power increases with signal strength — calibration gates noise without erasing true alignment.
- Raw max-aggregated CKA inflates with layer count; aggregation-aware calibration removes depth confounding; naive per-pair calibration is insufficient.
- **Global metrics after calibration**: CKA linear/RBF, RV, SVCCA, Procrustes lose scaling trends with model capacity; correlation with language-model ranking drops sharply (CKA linear: 0.86 → 0.45; Procrustes: 0.89 → 0.39).
- **Local metrics after calibration**: mKNN and CKNNA retain scaling trends; correlation with LM capability stays high (~0.85–0.87).
- Video–language: same pattern — calibrated CKA flat, calibrated mKNN scales with VideoMAE-Large/Huge.
- Locality ablation: topological agreement (neighbor identity) survives calibration; fine-grained metric agreement (small-bandwidth CKA-RBF) does not.

## Connections

- Directly challenges uncorrected CKA-based convergence claims, including those cited in [[convergent-world-representations-and-divergent-tasks|Convergent World Representations]] and the original Platonic Representation Hypothesis literature.
- Complements [[global-geometry-is-not-enough|Global Geometry Is Not Enough]]: global geometric statistics can mislead even when local functional structure is meaningful.
- Relevant to [[representation-geometry|Representation Geometry]] diagnostics, neuro-AI alignment, and transfer-learning model selection.
- Methodologically extends prior metric-specific CKA debiasing (Murphy et al., 2024; Chun et al., 2025) with a unified, aggregation-aware procedure.

## Limitations & Open Questions

> [!open-question]
> Why do representations converge in local neighborhoods but not in global geometry or exact local distances?

> [!open-question]
> Representational similarity has no ground-truth scale; calibration reports evidence above chance, not definitive proof of convergence.

> [!open-question]
> Exchangeability may fail for grouped or clustered samples; restricted permutations are needed in those settings.

> [!contradiction]
> Uncalibrated CKA scaling trends that motivated PRH largely disappear after calibration, refining — but not fully refuting — the original hypothesis since local convergence implies a weaker Aristotelian criterion.

## Future Work

- Explain why representations converge in local neighborhoods but not in global geometry or exact local distances — the authors identify this as the key open question behind the Aristotelian view.
- Extend calibrated convergence tests beyond exchangeable samples to grouped/clustered data via restricted permutations that preserve dependence structure.
